| 16 | 2026-10-21 | In TypeScript, money is a `number` holding a safe integer of kobo, not a `bigint`. `koboSchema` validates it; `nairaToKobo` and `koboToNaira` throw a `RangeError` on a fraction of a kobo or a value past `Number.MAX_SAFE_INTEGER`; pricing does its intermediate arithmetic in `bigint`. The query layer must read Postgres `bigint` columns into `number` and reject values past the safe range. Done in #18. | JSON has no `bigint`, so a `number` crosses the API, Zod and the web app unchanged. The safe range is about ₦90 trillion, far beyond any booking or payout. |
| 17 | 2026-10-21 | Pricing (PRD 6.1) rounds half up to whole kobo in exactly three places: the storage subtotal (`quantity × duration_days × unit_price_kobo`), the commission (`commission_rate × subtotal`) and the deposit (`deposit_percent × subtotal`). The rest is exact subtraction and addition: balance due = subtotal − deposit; provider payout = deposit − commission; charged online = deposit + service fee + delivery fee; platform revenue = commission + service fee + delivery margin. Quantities have at most 3 decimal places and rates at most 4 (whole basis points), and the arithmetic runs on integers, so a rate such as 0.075 causes no float error. Done in #18. | The PRD says "round to whole kobo" but not how. Deriving balance and payout by subtraction keeps deposit + balance equal to the subtotal and commission + payout equal to the deposit. Half up is what a farmer checking by hand expects. Rounding never reverses order, so with `deposit_percent > commission_rate` the payout is never negative. Three decimal places is a gram, or a thousandth of a sqm or crate. |
